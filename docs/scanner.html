How to read this page — how the ranking is scored, what every column measures, and what the page cannot tell you

The rank is a relative momentum score and nothing else. For each coin the scanner measures total return over four windows — 1, 3, 6 and 12 months (30, 91, 182 and 365 days). Each window is scored against the other 21 coins on the page (a percentile from 0 to 100), the four percentiles are averaged with equal weight, and the table is sorted on that average. Rank 1 is the strongest, rank 22 the weakest. A rank improves either because the coin rose or because its peers fell.

This is not the engine's ranking. The Crypto-Breadth book is chosen by the engine's own momentum, trend and liquidity rules at Monday's close. A coin can rank highly here and not be held, or be held and rank in the middle. The HELD tag marks what the book actually holds.

Percentile columns, written p0 to p100, compare a coin with its own past — 730 days of its own history, roughly 2 years — never with the other rows. RV p85 means today is more volatile than 85% of that coin's own past two years.

Directional signals

Where price and momentum point. Read these first.

Trend
Where price sits against its 50- and 200-day averages. Strong up = price above MA50 above MA200 with both averages rising; Up = above MA200 but the alignment is incomplete; Range = price between the two averages, or the two within 1% of each other; Down and Strong down mirror the two upward states.
Rank
The four-horizon momentum rank described above. 1 is strongest.
ΔR 28D
Change in that rank over the past 28 days. ↑ is climbing the table.
12-1%
The twelve-month return with the most recent month (30 days) left out, the standard momentum convention.
vs 52W hi
Distance below the highest daily close of the past 365 days. 0.0 is a fresh one-year closing high. The figure can never be positive.

Risk signals

These describe how large the moves are, not which way they go. A high reading is not bearish.

RV pctl
Realised volatility — 20 days of daily log returns, annualised by √365 — ranked against the coin's own history.
BBW pctl
Bollinger bandwidth: how wide the 20-day price band is, ranked the same way. RV below p25 and BBW below p10 together is a squeeze — a compressed range. It says nothing about the direction of the next move.
Squeeze release
Replaces Squeeze when a compressed coin moves more than 1.5 standard deviations of the 20 days before it. It says the quiet period broke, not which way or that it will continue.
ATR%
The average daily range over 14 days as a percentage of price — a sizing and stop-distance input.

State & confirmation

Today's context. None of these carries standalone predictive power; they confirm or contradict the directional zone.

1D%
The last UTC day's return.
Vol ×20D
The day's traded volume against its own 20-day average. Above 3× is flagged as an alert chip.
RS 1M
The past 30 days' log return minus BTC's over the same 30 days, in percentage points; positive means the coin outpaced bitcoin. With a single benchmark this orders the rows exactly as the raw one-month return does. Click the header to switch to the raw return. BTC's own row is blank.
Dev 200D
How far price sits above or below its 200-day average.

Crypto calendar and universe

Where this page differs from the ETF scanner it is ported from.

  • Crypto trades every day, so every look-back that means a span of time is converted to calendar days: 1M/3M/6M/12M = 30/91/182/365, 52 weeks = 365, the percentile window = 730. Look-backs quoted as a bar count are kept, as crypto charting quotes them: MA 20/50/200, RSI 14, ATR 14. MA200 therefore spans about 6.6 months here, against about 9.5 months on an equity chart.
  • The panel is the project's live Binance USDT universe. EOS, MATIC and LUNA are frozen in the data (rebranded or reassigned tickers) and are left off the page.
  • That universe is a fixed list chosen with hindsight: coins that faded out of the market before selection are absent, although some in it collapsed later (FTT). It suits a monitoring panel of current state; it would not suit a backtest.
  • There is no fund-wrapper layer: premium/discount and share-count flows have no spot-crypto counterpart.

Marks, colours and the strips above the table

  • Green is above zero, red below. An amber cell is an unusual risk reading — realised volatility above p80, or volume above 3× its 20-day average. Purple marks a squeeze.
  • A grey row ending in ● is more than 3 days behind the rest of the panel; it raises no alerts and is left out of the ranking, because its returns end on an older date than its peers'. A row inside that threshold but still behind carries a T-1 marker.
  • ^ means the percentile used a shorter window than the full one (a coin with under two years of history).
  • Overlays are kept separate on purpose. The gate chip carries two readings, each named by its basis: the breadth tier at the latest close, and the tier at the last Monday read, which is the one the book was last rebalanced to (it trades on the next day). The latest-close tier only acts if it still holds at the next Monday close; it is marked differs when the two disagree. The book chip is what is actually held. The ETH/BTC chip is the 50-day average of the ETH/BTC price ratio against its 200-day average — a display-only read of whether ether is leading bitcoin, used by nothing.
  • Alerts are events on the latest day: 52-week highs and lows, MA200 crossings, daily moves beyond 2 standard deviations, volume above 3× normal, squeezes and their releases, RSI at or beyond 75 / 25, and rows crossing into or out of the top 5 of the ranking. Click any chip to jump to that coin's row and open its chart.
  • Charts in sets one price basis for every row chart, remembered in this browser. USD is the default; BTC or ETH re-prices each chart as a ratio (the coin's close divided by bitcoin's or ether's on the same day), with MA50 and MA200 taken on the ratio. A rising ratio means the coin is outperforming that base, which is the chart form of the RS 1M column.
  • A rank crossing needs the row inside the top 5 today and outside it on each of the 7 days before, or the mirror. The cut is deliberately not 4, so it cannot be read as the engine's four-coin book.
  • A sigma move is one day against the standard deviation of the 20 daily returns before it; the window stops at the previous day so a large move cannot inflate its own yardstick.
  • The filter buttons narrow the table to the coins held in the book, or to those passing the engine's liquidity and history screen today (investable).

What this page is not

  • Monitoring, not a strategy: no buy list, no position sizes, no transaction costs, and no backtest behind any threshold shown.
  • Every parameter is an unvalidated industry default, converted to the crypto calendar. None has been tested on this universe.
  • Ranks are relative. Rank 1 in a falling market is still losing money — read it alongside 12-1% and Trend, which are absolute.
  • Nothing here changes what the Crypto-Breadth engine holds. The engine's rules are on the main dashboard.
